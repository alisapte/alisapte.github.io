Team project — commercial case

Taking a Rocket-Launched Glider to Market

The business side of the same glider project — sizing the market, pricing a self-assembly kit, and building the investment case to put it into production.
Same airframe as the engineering design study, reframed here as a product: a kit and a ready-built line aimed at hobbyists, families and STEM education.

00Overview

Once the glider itself was designed, my team and I built the case for actually manufacturing and selling it — sizing the market, working out unit economics for a self-assembly kit versus a ready-built model, and putting together an investment pitch to fund a first production run. Below is that commercial case: the product recap, the market opportunity, the numbers behind pricing and production, and how a £100,000 investment would be spent.

01The product

The design went through several rounds — from hand sketches to a laser-cut, tab-and-slot structure — before landing on a dihedral high-wing glider with a boom-mounted tail, sized to fly on a Klima C6-5 motor.

02Market opportunity

Balsa model gliders are a real but under-branded category — plenty of £30–£90 kits, but no clear leader combining build quality with a STEM angle.

05Sustainability

Wood is a renewable material to begin with, and the plan leaned further into that: recyclable packaging, compostable protective materials under consideration, and a design that's meant to survive repeated flights rather than get thrown away — with an obvious secondary life as a hands-on STEM teaching aid for physics and aerodynamics.
