Independent Coursework — Structural Optimisation

Optimising a Composite Wing Spar

A SolidWorks FEA and Design-of-Experiments study on the main spar of a Grob Astir CS sailplane wing — cutting material out of a heavily over-engineered baseline while keeping a safe margin against aerospace strength requirements.

Overview

The brief was to structurally analyse and optimise a wing spar for the Grob Astir CS sailplane: a tapered, hollow rectangular section running the 7.5 m half-span, built from carbon-fibre-reinforced polyester for its strength-to-weight ratio. A static FEA simulation modelled the spar under aerodynamic lift load, then a four-variable Taguchi L16 Design of Experiments was used to search for the lightest cross-section that still cleared a sensible aerospace factor of safety.

AircraftGrob Astir CS sailplane, 450 kg gross weight
Load case2,207 N per spar, uniform lift load
MaterialCFRP, 570 MPa yield

Baseline design

The as-given spar dimensions were run first to establish a starting point before any optimisation.

ParameterValue
Max von Mises stress40.12 MPa
Max displacement108.3 mm
Min factor of safety14.2

A factor of safety of 14.2 is roughly seven times the 1.5–2.0 typically used in aerospace structures — a clear sign the baseline spar was carrying far more material than it needed to, at a direct cost in weight and manufacturing cost.

Mesh dependency study

Before trusting any optimisation results, six mesh densities from 200 mm down to 15 mm element size were run to check the stress result had actually converged rather than being an artefact of a coarse mesh.

A 15 mm curvature-based mesh was carried forward into every subsequent simulation.

Design of experiments

Four cross-sectional dimensions were chosen as the design variables, each picked for a distinct structural reason rather than convenience:

VariableWhat it controlsLowHigh
Root top/bottom wall (RTBW)Second moment of area at the root, where bending moment peaks200 mm300 mm
Root side wall (RSW)Cross-section height — bending stiffness scales with height³100 mm160 mm
Top/bottom wall thickness (TWT)Material volume and bending resistance2.5 mm5 mm
Side wall thickness (SWT)Shear capacity, independent of TWT2.5 mm5 mm

A Taguchi L16 orthogonal array ran all 16 combinations under the same fixed-root, 2,207 N boundary condition, tracking peak stress, displacement and minimum factor of safety for each.

Optimised result

Run 1 — every variable at its lowest value — was selected as the optimum: it uses the least material of any configuration tested while still holding a factor of safety of 2.57, comfortably inside the aerospace target band.

ParameterBaselineOptimisedChange
Root top/bottom wall300 mm200 mm−33%
Root side wall160 mm100 mm−37%
Wall thicknesses5 mm2.5 mm−50%
Minimum factor of safety14.22.57−82%

The drop in factor of safety is the point, not a concern: it reflects deliberately stripping out the large structural margin the baseline carried, down to a value still safely inside the accepted range. The hollow CFRP section remains manufacturable with standard techniques at every tested wall thickness, and using less material lowers both cost and the embodied carbon of production.

Manufacturing drawing

The optimised spar was drawn up to third-angle projection with full root and tip cross-sections, ready to hand to manufacturing.
