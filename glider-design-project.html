Team project — design & build

Designing a Rocket-Launched Payload Glider

Concept generation, aerodynamic sizing and a build-ready structure for a small glider that launches on a model rocket motor and carries a 70 g payload back down in one piece.
Final design — dihedral high-wing glider with boom-mounted tail, sized around a NACA 0015 aerofoil and a Klima motor.

00Overview

The brief: design, build and test a small rocket-launched glider that carries an egg as a stand-in payload, survives the landing, and flies as far as possible — while staying buildable with hand tools and cheap enough to sell as a kit. My team and I (six of us) took it from a spread of hand-sketched concepts through a formal requirements and decision-matrix process, into an aerodynamically-sized final design, a laser-cut structure, and a written build guide. Below is that whole arc: the concept work, the sizing calculations behind the chosen shape, the structural detail, and the manufacturing and assembly side.

01Concept exploration

Six concepts came out of individual sketching, each pushing a different layout: dihedral high wings, boom-mounted tails, a snap-fit spine-and-rib fuselage, a simple box fuselage, and a streamlined powered-flight shape. Each was priced against a bill of materials before anything was scored.

02Requirements & decision

03Aerodynamic sizing

With Concept 1 selected, the airframe was sized around a NACA 0015 symmetrical aerofoil and a Klima C6-5 motor, then checked against take-off, climb, glide and stall performance.

Mass & aerofoil

Airframe, payload and motor mass set the wing loading; the aerofoil's lift and drag coefficients come from thin-aerofoil theory at the 5° cruise angle of attack.

C_L = aα   C_D = C_D,0 + KC_L²
Total mass (airframe + 70 g payload + motor)215 g
AerofoilNACA 0015, 5° AoA
C_L / C_D at Re = 50,0000.55 / 0.015

Take-off

Motor thrust sets the acceleration; take-off speed comes from the lift equation at the design C_L, and the equations of motion give distance and time to unstick.

V_to = √(2W/ρSC_L)   S = V_to²/2a
Motor (Klima C6-5)6 N, 1.6 s burn
Acceleration27.9 m/s²

Stability

Static margin comes from the neutral point relative to the CG — the mean aerodynamic chord and tail volume coefficient locate the neutral point first.

Climb, glide & stall

Climb velocity follows from thrust minus drag; after motor burnout the lift-to-drag ratio sets the glide angle, and the maximum-C_L point from the XFOIL polar sets the stall speed.

L/D = 1/tanθ = distance/height   V_stall = √(W/0.5ρSC_Lmax)

04Structure

Everything ties back to a tab-and-slot fuselage: a laser-cut box that only goes together one way, with an internal motor mount and thrust brace that also anchors the dihedral wing spars and stops the airframe racking under load.

06Build guide

The kit ships as 15 unique laser-cut and pre-cut parts — including 18 wing ribs, 4 wing spars and 2 tail booms — with a written sequence covering wing assembly, the tab-and-slot fuselage, solar-film covering and final tail installation.

The troubleshooting guide that ships with the kit covers the failure modes that showed up most in test builds:

IssueLikely causeFix
Wing panels wobbleSpar not fully seated; ribs loosePush spar fully through both chassis walls; dab cyano at each rib joint
Tailplane out of levelBooms unevenly seatedRe-seat both booms to the same length; check stabilisers are square
Egg shifts or rattlesBulkheads misaligned; bay too looseRealign bulkhead flats; pad gaps with soft tissue or foam
Solar film wrinkles or tearsUneven tension; sharp frame edgesRe-wrap from centre outward; sand burrs before recovering
Glider banks or rolls on launchUneven dihedral; skewed sparMatch wingtip heights; confirm dihedral blocks are identical
