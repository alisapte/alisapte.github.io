Designing the Main Landing Gear of the Airbus A350⁠-⁠1000 XWB

Touchdown speed, static load distribution, tyre selection and shock-absorber sizing, derived from public aircraft data under FAR 23/25.

00Overview

Landing gear sits at the intersection of structures, dynamics and certification: it has to absorb a heavy aircraft's full sink energy in a fraction of a second, within a regulated speed envelope, using off-the-shelf tyres. I picked the A350⁠-⁠1000 XWB and worked the whole chain from scratch — touchdown speed, weight distribution, tyre selection from a manufacturer's catalogue, and shock-absorber sizing — using only public aircraft data.

03Method & results

1 — Touchdown speed

FAR 23/25 bounds the design sink speed to 2.6–3.67 m/s. The wing-loading estimate below gives the "natural" sink speed the aircraft would tend toward; since it exceeds the regulatory band, the design value is capped at the upper limit.

V_T = 4.4 (W / Awing)0.25  [ft/s, W in lbf, A in ft²]
Natural touchdown speed4.63 m/s
Design touchdown speed (capped)3.67 m/s

2 — Static load distribution

Treating the fuselage as a rigid beam with point loads at the CG and both gear legs, taking moments about each gear gives the static share each leg carries — then divide by wheel count for per-wheel load.

Rmain = W·(xcg−xnose) / (xmain−xnose)
Static load / main wheel (×12)2.35 × 10⁵ N
Static load / nose wheel (×2)1.01 × 10⁵ N

4 — Landing energy

Vertical kinetic energy at touchdown is partly absorbed by tyre deflection and the rest by the shock absorbers. Tyre reaction force vs. deflection follows the catalogue's empirical curve; deflection is solved for the point where that force equals the per-wheel dynamic load (n × static).

KE = ½mvT²  ·  Z(δ) = 1.08Pw√(wD)·f(δ/w)
Total vertical KE at touchdown2.07 × 10⁶ J
Max. tyre deformation0.242 m
Work absorbed / tyre6.79 × 10⁴ J
Work absorbed / shock absorber6.30 × 10⁵ J

5 — Shock absorber sizing

Peak strut force comes straight from the static leg load, load factor and strut rake — efficiency only enters when converting absorbed energy into stroke, since it accounts for the strut not delivering a constant force over its travel.

Fmax = nWleg/cosθ   S = Eabs/(ηFmax)
Max. strut force3.53 × 10⁶ N
Max. strut stroke210 mm
